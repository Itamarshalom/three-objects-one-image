\begin{abstract}
Depth networks distort the Penrose triangle. We show that they do so because they must output a
depth map, and that a linear read-out of Depth Anything V2 gets the figure's corners and bars right. We build the triangle from
cubes, so its image is pixel for pixel the image of three valid objects, each with its gap hidden at a different
corner. Ten depth networks output none of them and bend the bars to flat or reversed slopes. The read-out,
fitted only on possible scenes, keeps each bar's slope until the last decoder stages. In
this read-out every bar comes nearer, by a total $T$ over one lap. It is known that a depth map cannot represent this, since depth must
return to its start after a lap. Erasing one corner, or only blocking attention to it (on the cubes erasure leaves visible), removes most of the bend.
Buildable rings of the same bars are mostly read correctly. We also train two heads on the same frozen features and scenes.
The depth head bends the bars, as the networks do. The depth-change head keeps them. Its bars come $0.96\,T$ nearer
per lap ($0.00$ on rings), and a cut at any corner gives the object with its gap there.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Each corner and bar of an impossible figure is a valid piece of a solid, and the conflict appears only around the
loop~\cite{huffman1971impossible,clowes1971seeing}. In one special view, an open chain of three bars of cubes looks like
the Penrose triangle, with its gap hidden at one of its corners (junctions J1--J3). If the bars are straight, the image has three valid readings, one per junction (Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}). Other solids give it
too~\cite{sugihara2000impossible}. Internally, the depth networks we probe read this figure as people
do~\cite{penrose1958impossible,kulpa1983impossible}. They read every junction as joined and every bar as coming nearer around the loop. These depth changes (a 1-form) sum to zero locally and to a total $T$ around the loop (in topology,
closed but not exact~\cite{penrose1992cohomology,dodik2025meschers}). Depth must return to its start after a lap, so a
depth map must cut the loop or bend the bars. A cut is one jump of $T$ (a tear), as at each valid object's gap. A bend
spreads the mismatch over the bars, and all ten networks we test do this.

It is known that no depth map can represent an impossible
object~\cite{penrose1992cohomology,akleman2023inconsistent,dodik2025meschers}. Our three tests show that trained
networks fail for this reason. A linear read-out of Depth Anything V2's features keeps every bar's slope until the last decoder
stages. In all ten networks, erasing one junction removes most of the bend. On one frozen encoder with only possible training
scenes, changing only the output from depth to depth changes keeps every bar's slope.
Also, a network's depth is close to a least-squares fit of its local readings.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Meschers~\cite{dodik2025meschers} stores an impossible object as a mesh 1-form with a nonzero sum around
the loop. It builds the object cut or bent (by least squares) and fits one to a Penrose triangle image. Its authors argue that people
see such objects locally. Akleman et~al.~\cite{akleman2023inconsistent} note that depth maps cannot represent such shapes
and non-conservative fields can. Depth networks have been probed with image
edits~\cite{vandijk2019how}, frozen-feature read-outs~\cite{elbanani2024probing,danier2025depthcues} and
illusions~\cite{yao2025illusion,nguyen2026mirage}. Jiang et~al.~\cite{jiang2019illusions} tested a depth network on
impossible figures qualitatively. CNN classifiers fail to tell possible from impossible
objects~\cite{heinke2021failure}.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Stimuli and measures}\label{sec:readout}
The loop is a chain of 15 unit cubes, five steps each along $+x$, $+y$ and $+z$. Seen orthographically along
$v=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3$, its end projects onto its start. Object $k$ has its gap at junction $k$ (corner cube $c_k$). Its cubes before $c_k$ move by $5(1,1,1)$, along $v$. So the three objects give one image, and
\mbox{$d_k=d_1-T\,\mathbf 1[c<c_k]$}, with $d_k$ object $k$'s depth, $c$ the cube index and $T=5\sqrt3$. At
the gap we trim the nearer bar's end by a prism parallel to $v$, so the three images are pixel-identical and each depth
map is exact. We render 288 impossible images (24 in-plane rotations $5^\circ$ apart, $\times$ 3 shadings $\times$ 2
textures $\times$ 2 chiralities), 48 possible controls (object $k$ untrimmed, gap visible) and 64 buildable closed rings
of the same bars with every junction visible (skew hexagons, rhombi). Probes and heads are trained only on 1500 random
possible scenes rendered like the stimuli, with one or two open chains of straight runs. They never contain the Penrose
family or a hidden gap that looks like a junction, so every contact in the image is one in 3D.

A cube's depth is the median output over its pixels at least 3\,px from its edge. We fit a line to each bar's four
interior cubes. A bar has the right sign if its slope has the objects' sign. The slope ratio $\rho$, a bar's slope on
the impossible image over that on its matched control, needs no calibration ($+1$ if unchanged, below 0 if reversed).
The jump $\Delta_j$ at junction $j$ is the next bar's line minus the previous one, at $j$. Most outputs are known only
up to shift and scale, so we measure each jump against the network's own controls. Let $r_j$ be its mean jump at $j$
on the two controls where $j$ is joined, and $g_j$ its jump on the control whose gap is at $j$. The excess tear
$e_j=(\Delta_j-r_j)/(g_j-r_j)$ is 0 for its ordinary step at a joined junction and 1 for its jump at a real gap. We
also give jumps in bar changes, where one bar change is an output's mean depth change along one bar (in this unit, $T=3$).
Rings have no gap controls (no $e_j$), so Fig.~\ref{fig:main}b uses this unit. The loop
integral $\Gamma$ is how much nearer the three bars come over one lap, junctions left out, so it tests only the bars.
For exact local depth changes it is $T$ on the impossible image and its controls, and 0 on rings.

\begin{proposition}[Least-squares fit on a loop]\label{sec:theory}
Fit one depth per cube to depth-change readings on the links of a loop of cubes, with weights $w_i>0$. If the readings
sum to $m$ around the loop, the fit's error $\epsilon_i$ on link $i$ is the share $(1/w_i)/\sum_l(1/w_l)$ of $m$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent (Minimise $\sum_iw_i\epsilon_i^2$ subject to $\sum_i\epsilon_i=m$. This is standard in depth from normals, where sparse ($L_1$) estimators cut the
loop~\cite{frankot1988enforcing,agrawal2006range,reddy2009enforcing,queau2018survey}.) Correct readings give $|m|=T$
on the impossible loop and $m=0$ on a ring. With equal weights every $\epsilon_i$ is $m/15$, the bent shape
of~\cite{dodik2025meschers}, so a link that reads $\delta_i$ keeps $\delta_i-m/15$. If all links read the same, the
bars are flat. If junction links read larger changes than bar links, bar links read less than $m/15$ and the bars
reverse. An erased junction (zero weight) gets the whole tear.
Any answer built from the objects cuts ($\sum_je_j=1$) but needs an accidental view~\cite{freeman1994generic}.

\subsection{Networks, probes and interventions}\label{sec:nets}
We run ten frozen networks: Depth Anything V2 S/B/L \mbox{(DA-S/B/L)}~\cite{yang2024dav2}, MiDaS
DPT-L~\cite{ranftl2021dpt,ranftl2022midas}, ZoeDepth~\cite{bhat2023zoedepth}, and, on the Stable Diffusion 2
backbone~\cite{rombach2022ldm}, Lotus-D/G~\cite{he2025lotus}, Marigold E2E-FT~\cite{garcia2025finetuning} and Marigold
v1.0/v1.1~\cite{ke2024marigold,ke2025marigold} (one denoising step). The generative ones run on 16 impossible images with several seeds. Marigold v1.1, E2E-FT and
Lotus-D also come as surface-normal networks, each with a depth twin on the same backbone. A normal is the local surface tilt. Like depth changes, normals need not
sum to zero around a loop. Ranges are over networks, and the repository lists each. \textit{Erasure} replaces junction
$j$ (corner cube and adjoining bar ends) by the exact background. \textit{Gluing} tests
Prop.~\ref{sec:theory} on network readings. It fits 15 cube depths by unweighted least squares to the mean
cube-to-cube changes (in bar changes) of its outputs on the three erased images (open loops).

\textit{Layer probes} are linear read-outs (ridge regressions) of the frozen tokens of DA-S and DA-B, at ViT blocks
L0--L12 and DPT fusion stages F1--F4. The depth-change probe predicts the exact local depth changes, and the depth
probe predicts depth. Each is fitted on 1200 of the scenes and calibrated by dividing its slopes and $\Gamma$ by the
share of the exact slope it recovers on the other 300. The \textit{attention cut} stops every token of Depth Anything
V2, in every encoder block, from attending to junction $j$'s ${\sim}88$ tokens on the intact image. The decoder still
uses them. The \textit{matched heads} are DPT decoders on frozen DA-S with an occlusion-edge channel, trained on 1400
of the scenes (100 to validate, two seeds each). They share data and schedule and differ only in the output and its
loss. The depth head outputs depth (its loss also matches depth changes), and the depth-change head outputs local
depth changes. Both see the whole image. On a head's
output, $\Gamma$ skips the pixels the head marks as edges. A least-squares fit with the loop cut at junction $k$ gives
the head's candidate for object $k$.

\section{Results}\label{sec:res}
\paragraph{Network outputs.}
No network cuts the loop (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}a). The total excess tear on the impossible image is $-0.19$ to $0.32$.
The mean bar slope is reversed in 0.96--1.00 of impossible images. On
controls it has the right sign in 0.92--1.00 for DA-S/B/L and Lotus-D and in 0.31--0.79 for the other six. The
networks tell a valid object from the impossible image junction by junction. A control's gap shows because the
near bar ends in front of the far one. All ${\sim}3{,}500$ pixels that differ from the impossible image lie at this
gap. The networks put their largest jump there in 0.88--1.00 of controls. In the impossible image every junction looks
joined, as in a valid object in the special view.

\paragraph{Erasure and closed rings.}
Erasing one junction of the Penrose loop grows the jump there by 2.13--5.16 bar changes ($T=3$, Fig.~\ref{fig:main}b),
and most bars regain the right sign \mbox{(0.58--1.00)}. Since the erased image is pixel-identical to an erased control, the
network cannot know which it sees. So the closed loop reverses the bars. The closed rings
are mostly read correctly (0.66--1.00 of the bars). Erasing a ring junction grows its jump by only 0.02--0.37, so the renders do not cause the reversal.

\paragraph{Layer probes.}
In DA-S and DA-B the depth-change probe finds almost no bar slope before block L6 (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}c), so the network computes the slopes that the probe finds later. From L7 to F1 the probe keeps the bars near their control's slope
($\rho=0.96$--$1.27$). Its $\Gamma$ is $1.08$--$1.28\,T$, as on the controls, and at most $0.31\,T$ on the rings. The
probe bends the bars at F4. The depth probe bends them at \mbox{L8--L12} ($\rho=0.10$--$0.91$) and in the decoder flattens
them (DA-B, ${\le}0.39$) or reverses them (DA-S, $-0.68$ to $-0.81$). On bar pixels identical in the impossible image
and its control, the tokens differ by 0.16--0.47 of their spread (L7--L12). There the depth-change
probe stays within 6\% of its value on the control (L8--L12). The depth probe falls to 0.28--0.69 of it.
The surface-normal networks also keep the bars' sign ($\rho=0.82$--$0.99$), but their depth twins reverse it.

\paragraph{Least-squares gluing.}
Gluing a network's outputs on the three erased images predicts its intact output with median Pearson $r=0.65$--$0.91$
(eight networks). The mean of the objects gives $0.16$--$0.66$, and the best object per image gives $0.50$--$0.76$. We
also tried junction weights from 0.01 to 100. Weight 1, the same as for bar links, fits best or within 0.002 of the
best. In all eight, junction links read larger changes than bar links, so the bars reverse.

\paragraph{Attention cut.}
On the intact image we measure only the cubes that erasure leaves visible. The tokens of $j$ still attend to all tokens. In \mbox{DA-S/B/L} the attention cut tears at $j$ (\mbox{$e_j=0.92$--$1.13$}, or $0.59$--$0.85$ counting $j$'s cubes),
and the bars regain the right sign \mbox{(0.74--1.00)}. At a joined junction of a control it gives only \mbox{$e=0.21$--$0.35$} (the
disturbance from the cut). So the mean $e$ of an image's three cuts separates impossible images from controls (AUC
1.00, with known junctions).

\paragraph{Matched heads.}
The depth-change head's bars sum to $\Gamma=0.96\,T$ around the loop and to $0.00$ around the rings
(Fig.~\ref{fig:main}d). Cutting its loop at junction $k$ gives the profile closest to object $k$ in every image. For a
depth map every cut gives the same profile, so its share is 1/3. The head does not show which junctions are joined.
Its edge channel leaves the loop closed on all 48 possible controls too, so $|\Gamma|$ cannot tell them from
impossible images (AUC 0.56--0.60). The depth networks tell them apart, as they find the controls' gaps. A depth map's
changes sum to 0 around any loop, so a depth head can only replace the bend with a cut. Trained in-domain like the
probes, our depth head is weak on bars even on controls (0.09--0.11 of the exact slope), and it flattens them. On the
impossible image they keep 0.39--0.47 of their slope on its controls (depth-change head: 1.00--1.01). At an impossible junction it makes only its ordinary step (median largest $e$: $0.06$, $-0.08$ for the
two seeds), at an erased junction it tears ($e_j=1.27$, $1.44$), and it finds the gap in 0.75--0.83 of controls.
DA-S's own decoder, on the same encoder, reverses the bars ($\rho=-0.97$, filled star in Fig.~\ref{fig:main}c).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}
A linear read-out of a network can get every junction and bar of this figure right. No depth map can represent
the figure, so a depth network must distort it. People also read the loop as closed~\cite{kulpa1983impossible} and still call the
figure impossible~\cite{penrose1958impossible}. Our depth-change head does the first. Its edge channel does not show
which junctions are joined. A network that outputs depth changes and a map of where the surface is joined could do both.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We test only orthographic renders of one figure at one mismatch size, out of distribution for every network. We use linear probes on
two networks and heads on one backbone (two seeds).
